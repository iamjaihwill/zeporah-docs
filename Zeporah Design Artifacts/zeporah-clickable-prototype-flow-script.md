# Zeporah Clickable Prototype Flow Script

This document translates the low-fidelity wireframe package into a clickable prototype script for the first end-to-end MVP journey. It is intended to guide prototype linking, walkthrough testing, and early stakeholder review before mid-fidelity design begins.[cite:17][cite:9][cite:12][cite:26]

The prototype should focus on one believable family journey rather than trying to simulate the entire product. The most valuable first path is a new Family Coordinator entering Zeporah after a recent loss, setting up a private space, arriving at the home dashboard, opening a priority task, feeling overwhelmed, using compassionate pause, and requesting human support through explicit consent.[cite:17][cite:12][cite:9]

## Prototype objective

The clickable prototype should prove five things in one coherent journey.[cite:26][cite:17][cite:12]

- Zeporah can establish trust quickly.[cite:9]
- The onboarding flow is calm and not overly demanding.[cite:17][cite:16]
- The home screen reduces complexity into a few guided next steps.[cite:17]
- A difficult task can be paused without shame or loss of work.[cite:12]
- A support request can be made through clear, limited, explicit sharing.[cite:12][cite:9]

## Primary persona for the prototype

The first clickable prototype should be built around the Family Coordinator because the current MVP materials place that role at the center of onboarding, task orchestration, privacy control, and external support consent.[cite:17][cite:9][cite:12]

**Persona setup for prototype narration:**
- Recently lost a parent.[cite:17]
- Feels overwhelmed by practical next steps.[cite:12][cite:16]
- Wants one private place to organize what comes next.[cite:17]
- May need help from one trusted family member and possibly from a support resource later.[cite:16][cite:12]

8. LF-10 Choose role.[cite:9]
9. LF-11 Minimal setup.[cite:9][cite:17]
10. LF-12 What feels most helpful right now.[cite:9]
11. LF-13 Name workspace.[cite:9]

## Frame-by-frame linking script

### 1. Welcome to privacy

**Start frame:** LF-01 Welcome / direct entry.[cite:17]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Create a private family space** moves to LF-04 Privacy overview.[cite:9]
- Clicking **Learn how privacy works** also moves to LF-04, ideally as a secondary path.[cite:9]
- Clicking **I already have an account** may remain out of scope for this first prototype branch or open a dead-end note frame labeled “returning-user sign-in path not included in v1 walkthrough.”[cite:26]

**Prototype note:**
This first click should feel decisive and calm, not like a funnel start screen.[cite:16][cite:17]

### 2. Privacy to setup preferences

**Current frame:** LF-04 Privacy overview.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Continue** moves to LF-05 Language and accessibility.[cite:9]
- Clicking **Read the full privacy summary** can open an overlay or side sheet in the prototype rather than a full additional screen.[cite:9]

**Prototype note:**
The privacy explanation should be short enough that stakeholders can see the trust model in one glance.[cite:9]

### 3. Preferences to secure entry

**Current frame:** LF-05 Language and accessibility.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Save and continue** moves to LF-06 Create account / sign in.[cite:9]
- Clicking **Skip for now** also moves to LF-06.[cite:9]

### 4. Secure entry path

**Current frame:** LF-06 Create account / sign in.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Selecting email or phone and clicking **Continue** moves to LF-07 Verification code.[cite:9]
- Clicking **I already have an account** can route to an out-of-scope note frame for prototype completeness.[cite:26]

### 5. Verification to capability boundary

**Current frame:** LF-07 Verification code.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Verify** moves to LF-08 What Zeporah can and cannot do.[cite:9]
- **Resend code** and **Use a different email or number** may be non-navigating prototype affordances or tiny note overlays.[cite:26]

### 6. Boundary-setting to create/join branch

**Current frame:** LF-08 What Zeporah can and cannot do.[cite:9][cite:17]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **I understand, continue** moves to LF-09 Start or join workspace.[cite:9]

**Prototype note:**
This step is important because it frames support and urgent-help boundaries before later support interactions occur.[cite:12][cite:9]

### 7. Workspace creation branch

**Current frame:** LF-09 Start or join workspace.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Start a new family space** moves to LF-10 Choose role.[cite:9]
- A join-existing option can remain visible but not used in the first guided path.[cite:26]

### 8. Role choice

**Current frame:** LF-10 Choose role.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Selecting **Coordinator** and clicking **Continue** moves to LF-11 Minimal setup.[cite:9]
- Selecting **Not sure yet** may still route to LF-11 with a prototype note that the default branch behaves like Coordinator for walkthrough purposes.[cite:26]

### 9. Minimal setup

**Current frame:** LF-11 Minimal setup.[cite:9][cite:17]

**Recommended prototype inputs:**
- Loved one name: “Mom” or “Angela.”
- Current stage: “Recent loss.”
- Relationship: “Parent.”
- Location: Georgia zip placeholder.[cite:17]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Continue** moves to LF-12 What feels most helpful right now.[cite:9]

### 10. Immediate-need selection

**Current frame:** LF-12 What feels most helpful right now.[cite:9]

**Recommended selected cards:**
- Knowing what to do first.[cite:17]
- Keeping information in one place.[cite:17]
- Finding support or someone to talk to.[cite:12]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Continue** moves to LF-13 Name workspace.[cite:9]

### 11. Workspace naming

**Current frame:** LF-13 Name workspace.[cite:9]

**Recommended prototype input:**
- Workspace name: “Mom’s next steps” or “Family next steps.”[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Continue** moves to LF-14 Privacy baseline.[cite:9]

### 12. Privacy baseline confirmation

**Current frame:** LF-14 Privacy baseline.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Keep these private defaults** moves to LF-15 Privacy receipt.[cite:9]
- The customize path can remain visible but unused in the first walkthrough.[cite:26]

### 13. Privacy receipt to collaboration prompt

**Current frame:** LF-15 Privacy receipt.[cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Continue** moves to LF-16 Invite helper now or later.[cite:9]

### 14. Helper decision

**Current frame:** LF-16 Invite helper now or later.[cite:9]

**Recommended first-path choice:**
- Choose **I’ll do this later** to keep the first prototype focused.[cite:26]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Continue** after selecting later moves to LF-18 Support preferences.[cite:9]

**Optional alternate branch:**
- Choosing **Invite someone now** can go to LF-17 Invitation preview if you want a second demo branch.[cite:9]

### 15. Support preferences

**Current frame:** LF-18 Support preferences.[cite:9][cite:12]

**Recommended prototype selections:**
- Either local or virtual.
- English.
- No faith preference selected.
- Lower-cost preferred.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Save and continue** moves to LF-20 Setup review / calm completion.[cite:9]

### 16. Completion to first dashboard

**Current frame:** LF-20 Setup review / calm completion.[cite:17][cite:16]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Go to my next step** moves to LF-21 Home / recent loss.[cite:17]
- Clicking **Save and leave** can route to a simple note frame or be left inactive for this walkthough.[cite:26]

### 17. Dashboard to first guided card

**Current frame:** LF-21 Home / recent loss.[cite:17]

**Recommended card order for prototype:**
1. Tell the people who need to know.
2. Save important contacts.
3. Keep documents in one place.
4. Ask someone to help with one thing.[cite:17][cite:16]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking the first priority card moves to LF-24 First things first card detail.[cite:17]
- A Find support quick action may optionally jump to LF-51 for secondary demonstration.[cite:12]

### 18. Guided card to task detail

**Current frame:** LF-24 First things first card detail.[cite:17]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Start this step** moves to LF-33 Task detail.[cite:17][cite:9]
- Clicking **Show me a smaller step** could jump directly to LF-48 in an alternate branch, though the main journey should continue through task detail first.[cite:12]

### 19. Task detail to compassionate pause

**Current frame:** LF-33 Task detail.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Prototype narrative trigger:**
The user opens a task, begins to process it, and feels overwhelmed by the practical/emotional weight of it.[cite:12][cite:16]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Show me one smaller step** may go to LF-48 in an alternate branch.[cite:12]
- Clicking **Save and come back later** may go to LF-50 in an alternate branch.[cite:12]
- For the main prototype, clicking **See support options** or **I need help** should open LF-47 Compassionate pause menu first.[cite:12]

### 20. Compassionate pause decision point

**Current frame:** LF-47 Compassionate pause menu.[cite:12]

**Main-path interaction:**
- Clicking **See support options** moves to LF-51 Support options.[cite:12]

**Optional branches:**
- **Show me one smaller step** → LF-48.[cite:12]
- **Ask someone I trust to help** → LF-49.[cite:12]
- **Save and come back later** → LF-50.[cite:12]
- **I need urgent help** → LF-57.[cite:12]

**Prototype note:**
This is the emotional pivot of the MVP and should be reviewed carefully in stakeholder sessions.[cite:12][cite:16]

### 21. Support options to connection request

**Current frame:** LF-51 Support options.[cite:12]

**Recommended prototype card selection:**
- A support resource aligned to emotional or practical help, depending selected need.[cite:12]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Request connection** on a resource card moves to LF-53 Request a connection.[cite:12]
- Clicking **Contact independently** can remain visible but unused in the first walkthrough.[cite:12]

### 22. Request a connection form

**Current frame:** LF-53 Request a connection.[cite:12]

**Recommended prototype inputs:**
- Request type: caregiver or bereavement support, or practical caregiver support.[cite:12]
- Preferred contact method: phone.[cite:12]
- Preferred contact time: afternoon.[cite:12]
- Optional message: brief help request sentence.[cite:12]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Review what will be shared** moves to LF-54 Share preview.[cite:12]

### 23. Share preview and consent

**Current frame:** LF-54 Share preview.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Send my request** moves to LF-55 Request sent.[cite:12]
- Clicking **Edit request** returns to LF-53.[cite:12]

**Prototype note:**
Stakeholders should be able to see the trust difference between the “will share” and “will not share” columns instantly.[cite:12][cite:9]

### 24. Confirmation state

**Current frame:** LF-55 Request sent.[cite:12]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **View request status** moves to LF-56 Request status.[cite:12]
- Clicking **Return to my task** returns to LF-33 Task detail or LF-21 Home, depending the prototype branch choice.[cite:12][cite:17]

### 25. Request status

**Current frame:** LF-56 Request status.[cite:12]

**Interaction:**
- Clicking **Return to my task** returns to LF-33 Task detail.[cite:12]
- Clicking **See other support options** returns to LF-51.[cite:12]

**Prototype note:**
This screen should remain operationally sparse; it is about transparency, not detailed case management.[cite:12]

### 26. End-state return

**End frame:** LF-33 Task detail or LF-21 Home / recent loss.[cite:12][cite:17]

**Success condition for walkthrough:**
The reviewer should understand that the user has preserved work, stayed in control of privacy, and successfully moved from overwhelm to supported next steps without the app overreaching.[cite:12][cite:9][cite:16]

## Optional branch set

Once the main linear walkthrough is stable, the prototype can add three optional branches without broadening the scope too much.[cite:26]

- **Branch A:** Invite helper during onboarding, using LF-16 → LF-17.[cite:9]
- **Branch B:** Show me one smaller step from LF-47 to LF-48 instead of seeking support.[cite:12]
- **Branch C:** I need urgent help from LF-47 to LF-57 to demonstrate emergency boundaries.[cite:12]

## Prototype testing script

The prototype should be tested using a consistent facilitator script so feedback stays focused on product questions rather than visual polish.[cite:26]

### Facilitator setup

Use a simple scenario: “You recently lost a parent and need a private place to keep track of what needs to happen next. You are not ready to deal with everything at once.”[cite:17][cite:16]

### Questions to answer during review

- Does the product feel trustworthy within the first three screens?[cite:9]
- Does onboarding ask for the right amount of information?[cite:17][cite:9]
- Does the home screen make the next step feel smaller?[cite:17]
- Does the pause flow feel humane rather than alarming or generic?[cite:12][cite:16]
- Is the support request preview clear enough that a user would know exactly what is being shared?[cite:12][cite:9]

## Completion criteria

The first clickable prototype journey should be considered ready when the following are true.[cite:26][cite:9][cite:12]

- All 26 linked steps in the main path function in sequence.[cite:26]
- The prototype can be narrated without needing verbal explanation of missing trust cues.[cite:9]
- The emotional transition from task overwhelm to support request feels intentional and calm.[cite:12][cite:16]
- The prototype demonstrates explicit consent and limited sharing clearly enough for stakeholder review.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Reviewers can identify the product's core differentiation from a generic task manager or support marketplace.[cite:17][cite:12][cite:16]
