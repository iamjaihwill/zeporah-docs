# Zeporah Low-Fidelity Home, Tasks, and Support Content Brief

This document defines the frame-by-frame low-fidelity content structure for Zeporah's Home and guidance screens, Tasks and updates screens, and Compassionate pause and support screens.[cite:17][cite:12][cite:9]

These areas are the core of the MVP because they determine whether Zeporah feels like a clear, calm family operating system instead of a generic checklist or resource marketplace.[cite:17][cite:16][cite:12]

## Usage rules

Each frame should include a side annotation block with purpose, primary role, primary action, trust cues, key branches, and any role restrictions.[cite:26]

These wireframes should preserve the same interaction posture established in onboarding: small next steps, plain language, explicit access boundaries, and no automatic support outreach or silent sharing.[cite:9][cite:12][cite:16]

## Home and guidance

### LF-21 Home / recent loss

**Purpose:** Provide a calm first dashboard focused on the most immediate responsibilities after a loss without flooding the user with the full roadmap.[cite:17]

**Frame structure:**
- Header: workspace name, small stage label, profile or settings affordance.[cite:17][cite:9]
- Reassurance banner: “You do not need to do everything today.”[cite:16][cite:17]
- Short supporting text: “Start with a few things that matter now. Other tasks can wait.”[cite:17]
- Section 1: What matters now, showing 3 to 5 priority cards max.[cite:17][cite:9]
- Suggested cards may include:
  - Tell the people who need to know.[cite:17]
  - Save important contacts.[cite:17]
  - Keep documents in one place.[cite:17]
  - Ask someone to help with one thing.[cite:16][cite:9]
- Section 2: Later, collapsed or de-emphasized cards for upcoming responsibilities.[cite:17]
- Section 3: Quick actions such as Invite helper, Add document, Find support.[cite:9][cite:12]
- Bottom navigation or persistent primary nav.[cite:9]

**Trust cues:**
- “Only people you invite can see shared items.”[cite:9]
- “Nothing is sent to an organization unless you review and send it.”[cite:12][cite:9]

**Primary action:** Open the top priority card.[cite:17]

### LF-22 Home / life after loss

**Purpose:** Shift the dashboard into a longer-horizon support and administration mode after the immediate period has passed.[cite:17]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with workspace name and stage label.[cite:17]
- Reassurance banner: “You can take this one step at a time.”[cite:16][cite:17]
- Section 1: Continue now, with 3 to 5 priority cards focused on follow-through items.[cite:17]
- Example cards may include:
  - Review accounts and subscriptions.[cite:17]
  - Track expenses and reimbursements.[cite:17]
  - Follow up on decisions you already made.[cite:17][cite:16]
  - Update your support plan.[cite:12]
- Section 2: Keep organized, linking into documents, notes, contacts, and decision log.[cite:17][cite:16]
- Section 3: Support and reminders, with gentle paths back into help.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Trust cues:**
- “Zeporah can help you keep track without sharing your information automatically.”[cite:9]

**Primary action:** Open top follow-through priority.[cite:17]

### LF-23 Welcome-back state

**Purpose:** Help returning users re-enter without guilt after time away.[cite:9][cite:16]

**Frame structure:**
- Header and workspace name.[cite:9]
- Main message: “Welcome back. You can pick up where you left off.”[cite:16][cite:9]
- Supporting line: “A few things are ready when you are.”[cite:16]
- Section showing:
  - One resumed task.[cite:9]
  - One new or upcoming item.[cite:9]
  - One support or helper action, if relevant.[cite:12]
- Quiet action row: Start with today, Review changes, Not now.[cite:9]

**Trust cue:**
- “Paused items stay private and can be recovered later.”[cite:12]

### LF-24 First things first card detail

**Purpose:** Expand one roadmap card into a simple, confidence-building task introduction.[cite:17]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to Home.[cite:17]
- Main headline matching selected card title.[cite:17]
- Why this matters block in one short paragraph.[cite:17]
- What you can do now block with 2 to 4 small actions.[cite:17][cite:16]
- What can wait block to reduce urgency pressure.[cite:17]
- Optional help block: ask someone, save for later, find support.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Start this step.[cite:17]
- Secondary CTA: Show me a smaller step.[cite:12]

### LF-25 Stage switch confirmation

**Purpose:** Let the coordinator change the journey stage without fear of losing work or exposing information.[cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:9]
- Main headline: “Change where Zeporah guides you next?”[cite:9]
- Current stage summary.[cite:17]
- New stage summary.[cite:17]
- What stays the same list: people, private information, saved tasks, support settings.[cite:9]
- What may change list: priority cards, suggested tasks, reminders.[cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Confirm stage change.[cite:9]
- Secondary CTA: Keep current stage.[cite:9]

## Tasks and updates

### LF-32 Task list

**Purpose:** Give the family a central queue for work that is understandable by time horizon and status.[cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header: title plus add-task action.[cite:9]
- Tabs or segment controls: Today, This week, Later, Completed.[cite:9]
- Optional filter row: All, Mine, Shared, Private, Needs help.[cite:9][cite:12]
- Task rows should include:
  - Task title.[cite:9]
  - Assignee or unassigned status.[cite:9]
  - Due timing.[cite:9]
  - Visibility label.[cite:9]
  - Progress state.[cite:9]
- Empty state version: “There is nothing here right now. Add a task or start from Home.”[cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Open selected task.[cite:9]
- Secondary CTA: Add task.[cite:9]

**Trust cue:**
- Visibility chip on every task row.[cite:9]

### LF-33 Task detail

**Purpose:** Let the user complete, delegate, pause, or make a task smaller without losing context.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to task list or source screen.[cite:9]
- Task title and visibility chip.[cite:9]
- Summary block: why this task exists, due timing, owner, role-specific context.[cite:9][cite:17]
- Main content section may contain checklist items, linked notes, or related documents placeholder.[cite:17][cite:9]
- Action row:
  - Mark complete.[cite:9]
  - Ask someone to help.[cite:12]
  - Show me one smaller step.[cite:12]
  - Save and come back later.[cite:12]
- Optional related items block: contacts, documents, updates, next tasks.[cite:17]

**Trust cues:**
- “Who can see this” disclosure line.[cite:9]
- “Related notes and documents remain private unless you share them separately.”[cite:12][cite:9]

### LF-34 Create or edit task

**Purpose:** Add or update structured work without forcing too much detail.[cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with cancel and save.[cite:9]
- Main title: New task or Edit task.[cite:9]
- Fields:
  - Task title.[cite:9]
  - Optional notes.[cite:9]
  - Due timing.[cite:9]
  - Category.[cite:17]
  - Visibility setting.[cite:9]
  - Assignee, optional.[cite:9]
- Inline helper copy for visibility choices.[cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Save task.[cite:9]
- Secondary CTA: Save draft or Cancel.[cite:9]

### LF-35 Assign task

**Purpose:** Delegate one specific task with clear scope and no accidental over-sharing.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Ask someone to help with this task.”[cite:12]
- Existing people list first.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Optional path to invite someone for this one task.[cite:12]
- Permission summary beneath selection: task-only visibility unless expanded intentionally.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Review assignment.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Save draft request.[cite:12]

### LF-36 Assignment preview

**Purpose:** Confirm the exact scope of delegated access before the task is sent.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Review what they will see.”[cite:12]
- Recipient block.[cite:9]
- Task summary block.[cite:9]
- Two-column preview:
  - They will be able to see or do.[cite:12][cite:9]
  - They will not be able to see or do.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Optional personal note preview.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Send assignment.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Edit access.[cite:9]

### LF-37 Updates feed

**Purpose:** Give the family a low-noise place to share and review selected updates.[cite:9][cite:16]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with title and write-update action.[cite:9]
- Feed items showing message snippet, time, and intended audience label.[cite:9]
- Empty state version for new spaces.[cite:9]
- Filter row optional: All updates, My updates, Shared with me.[cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Open or write update.[cite:9]

**Trust cue:**
- Every post shows intended audience label.[cite:9]

### LF-38 Write family update

**Purpose:** Let the coordinator or authorized collaborator draft a clean, audience-aware update.[cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with cancel and preview.[cite:9]
- Main title: “Write an update.”[cite:9]
- Text area.[cite:9]
- Optional audience selector.[cite:9]
- Optional include task reference toggle.[cite:9]
- Note: “Private notes are separate and not included here.”[cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Preview audience.[cite:9]
- Secondary CTA: Save draft.[cite:9]

### LF-39 Update audience preview

**Purpose:** Confirm exactly who will receive the family update before sharing.[cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:9]
- Main headline: “Review who will see this update.”[cite:9]
- Message preview snippet.[cite:9]
- Recipient groups list.[cite:9]
- Not included list for excluded roles or individuals.[cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Share update.[cite:9]
- Secondary CTA: Edit audience.[cite:9]

## Compassionate pause and support

### LF-47 Compassionate pause menu

**Purpose:** Create a humane interruption point whenever the user cannot continue.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Modal, tray, or full-screen card depending context.[cite:12]
- Title: “You can pause here.”[cite:12]
- Body copy: “This is a lot to carry. Your progress is saved. Choose what feels most possible next.”[cite:12]
- Action list:
  - Show me one smaller step.[cite:12]
  - Ask someone I trust to help.[cite:12]
  - See support options.[cite:12]
  - Save and come back later.[cite:12]
  - Hide this for now.[cite:12]
  - I need urgent help.[cite:12]
- Soft close or cancel action.[cite:12]

**Trust cues:**
- “No one is contacted from this screen.”[cite:12]
- “Your work is already saved.”[cite:12]

### LF-48 Smaller-step planner

**Purpose:** Break one difficult action into one manageable next move.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to pause menu.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Let’s make this smaller.”[cite:12]
- One-line encouragement: “One step is enough for now.”[cite:12]
- Up to three suggested smaller actions, context-dependent.[cite:12]
- Alternate controls: Show different options, Break into smaller steps, Save and return later.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Choose this step.[cite:12]

### LF-49 Ask someone I trust

**Purpose:** Let the user request narrow help from another person without broadening access by accident.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to pause menu.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “You do not have to do this alone.”[cite:12][cite:16]
- Section 1: People already in my family space.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Section 2: Invite someone for this one task.[cite:12]
- Section 3: Write a private request I can send myself.[cite:12]
- Optional branch link: I do not have anyone to ask.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Continue with selected option.[cite:12]

**Trust cue:**
- “You decide what they can see.”[cite:12][cite:9]

### LF-50 Save / hide / return later

**Purpose:** Preserve work and give the user emotional space without deleting anything.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to pause menu.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Take the time you need.”[cite:12][cite:16]
- Body text: “Your work is saved. Choose when, if ever, you want this to come back.”[cite:12]
- Choice list:
  - Do not remind me.[cite:12]
  - Ask me tomorrow.[cite:12]
  - Ask me next week.[cite:12]
  - Ask me in a month.[cite:12]
  - I will bring it back myself.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Save preference.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Return without changing reminder.[cite:12]

**Trust cue:**
- “Hidden items stay private and can be found later.”[cite:12]

### LF-51 Support options

**Purpose:** Show a limited, transparent set of support choices without creating a referral marketplace feel.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to pause menu or source screen.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Support that may help right now.”[cite:12]
- Optional need selector if context is unclear.[cite:12]
- Up to four resource cards only.[cite:12]
- Each resource card should contain:
  - Resource name.[cite:12]
  - Support category.[cite:12]
  - What it can help with.[cite:12]
  - Why am I seeing this?[cite:12]
  - Relationship label such as hospice team, organization-approved resource, public resource, nonprofit, local provider, virtual option.[cite:12]
  - Cost status where verified.[cite:12]
  - Optional language or accessibility markers.[cite:12]
- Card actions: Save, Hide, Contact independently, Request connection.[cite:12]

**Trust cues:**
- “Nothing is shared unless you choose to send a request.”[cite:12]
- “Recommendations are not ranked by paid placement.”[cite:12]

### LF-52 Resource detail

**Purpose:** Expand one support option with enough information for a confident next action.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back to support options.[cite:12]
- Resource title and category.[cite:12]
- What this can help with.[cite:12]
- Why it may fit your situation.[cite:12]
- How to contact or request connection.[cite:12]
- What Zeporah will not share automatically.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Contact independently or Request connection.[cite:12]

### LF-53 Request a connection

**Purpose:** Gather only the approved information needed to initiate human support.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Request a connection.”[cite:12]
- Fields:
  - Request type.[cite:12]
  - Preferred contact method.[cite:12]
  - Preferred contact time.[cite:12]
  - Optional short message.[cite:12]
  - Preferred language, optional.[cite:12]
  - Voicemail permission, optional.[cite:12]
- Role note if not coordinator: may need coordinator approval.[cite:12][cite:9]
- Primary CTA: Review what will be shared.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Save draft.[cite:12]

### LF-54 Share preview

**Purpose:** Show the exact information that will and will not be transmitted before consent.[cite:12][cite:9]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Review this request before sending.”[cite:12]
- Recipient block with named organization or support recipient.[cite:12]
- Will be shared column.[cite:12]
- Will not be shared column.[cite:12]
- Consent checkbox or acknowledgment line.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Send my request.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Edit request.[cite:12]

### LF-55 Request sent

**Purpose:** Confirm that the support request was sent without promising outcomes beyond configured status visibility.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with close or return path.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Your request was sent.”[cite:12]
- Summary card of recipient and request category.[cite:12]
- Small note: “If status updates are available, you will see them here.”[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: View request status.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Return to my task.[cite:12]

### LF-56 Request status

**Purpose:** Show limited operational follow-up without exposing staff notes or clinical information.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Header with back.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Your support request.”[cite:12]
- Timeline or status blocks such as Received, Team notified, Attempted contact, Connected, Complete, Unavailable.[cite:12]
- Actions where allowed:
  - Review what I shared.[cite:12]
  - Update contact preference.[cite:12]
  - Withdraw request, if still possible.[cite:12]
  - See other support options.[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Return to my task.[cite:12]

**Trust cue:**
- “Internal staff notes are not shown here.”[cite:12]

### LF-57 Urgent-help boundary

**Purpose:** Set a hard, clear boundary when the user explicitly signals urgent help need.[cite:12]

**Frame structure:**
- Full-screen alert-style wireframe or focused modal.[cite:12]
- Main headline: “Get urgent help now.”[cite:12]
- Primary message: “If someone is in immediate danger or needs urgent medical help, call local emergency services now.”[cite:12]
- Hospice-specific line shown only if configured and verified.[cite:12]
- Product boundary line: “Zeporah cannot monitor emergencies, provide emergency response, or decide what level of care is needed.”[cite:12]
- Primary CTA: Call emergency services.[cite:12]
- Secondary CTA: Call hospice line, if available.[cite:12]
- Tertiary CTA: Return to Zeporah.[cite:12]

## Review checklist

These wireframes should be considered ready for integration when the following are true.[cite:26][cite:9][cite:12]

- Home screens show only a few priority cards, not the full universe of work.[cite:17]
- Task detail always includes paths to delegate, reduce, or pause work.[cite:12]
- Support screens never imply automatic contact, diagnosis, or monitoring.[cite:12]
- Every task and support-sensitive area clearly communicates visibility or sharing rules.[cite:9][cite:12]
- The overall tone remains calm, practical, and dignified rather than urgent or transactional.[cite:16][cite:17]
