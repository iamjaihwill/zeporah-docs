## 2. The venture in brief

**Problem.** After a death, and often well before it, one family member becomes the de facto coordinator of funeral arrangements, documents, death certificates, family communication, benefits, insurance, property, and deadlines. The relevant help is fragmented across funeral homes, attorneys, insurers, agencies, and relatives; no one coordinates the whole journey, which can run 12–24 months.

**Product.** A private, role-based family workspace that tells the coordinator what matters now and what can wait, lets trusted helpers take on specific work without seeing everything, keeps documents and decisions in one controlled place, and points to trustworthy resources.

**North Star:** *"I finally know what to do next—and I do not have to do it alone."*

**Origin.** The brand story is grounded in the founder's aunt, Annie Anita April, diagnosed with stage 4 cancer in 2024, and the family's experience of searching for answers while grieving.

## 4. Product definition

### 4.1 MVP (One-Page Brief and five-workflow deck)

Georgia-first, metro-Atlanta launch, centered on the **Family Coordinator**. The deck frames the MVP as five "moments that matter," built in this order:

| # | Workflow | User's question | Proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First Things First | "I do not know what to do first." | Immediate clarity (first plan in under 10 minutes; 3–5 priority cards) |
| 2 | Family Circle | "How can people help without chaos?" | Coordination without conflict (4–5 helpers, role-based, revocable) |
| 3 | Documents & Decisions | "Where do we keep everything?" | Lasting practical value (per-item visibility, expense ledger, decision log) |
| 4 | Resource & Support Finder | "Who do I call?" | Trusted help (official sources first, disclosed economics) |
| 5 | Keep Moving Forward | "What is still left—and what can wait?" | Respectful follow-through (no countdowns, streaks, or shame) |

### 4.2 Non-negotiables

- Organization, general information, and referrals only — never legal, tax, financial, medical, or funeral-director advice.
- Never determine legal authority, interpret legal documents, resolve disputes, sell at-need funeral services, or hold family funds.
- No SSNs, bank details, full policy numbers, estate values, or unnecessary medical data at onboarding.
- Calm language and optional reminders; no shame-based progress, countdowns, or aggressive upsells.
- Family data private by default; access coordinator-controlled, logged, and revocable.

### 4.3 Year-one proof of success (Brief)

60%+ of started cases complete onboarding · 40%+ complete three priority actions within seven days · 50%+ invite a helper · 70%+ report reduced confusion · zero serious privacy, undisclosed-fee, or legal-boundary incidents · 100–300 activated Georgia cases · 20–60 paid packages · 5–10 referral partners · one hospice pilot, LOI, or referral relationship.

## 5. The six workflow specifications

All six share a release context — **hospice and palliative-care pilot** — and the same architecture: purpose and value (family and institutional), participants, entry triggers, screen-by-screen flow with exact copy, branching logic, permissions, notifications, pause and safety behavior, partner operations, edge cases, data minimization, metrics, audit events, acceptance criteria, and an explicit handoff section.

| WF | Title | Version | Core idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Family Onboarding, Consent, Roles, Permissions, and Visual Trust System | 1.3 | Creates the private workspace, roles, privacy defaults, and consent ledger every later workflow inherits |
| 2 | Serious Illness and Hospice-Care Preparation | 1.2 | "Now, Next, Later" care plan: contacts, care-team questions, family notes, values, practical tasks, caregiver check-in |
| 3 | Family Coordination, Communication, Tasks, and Documents | 1.0 | Controlled "Family Updates" instead of group chat; task-only delegation; item-level document access |
| 4 | Caregiver Overwhelm, Compassionate Pause, and Human-Support Escalation | 1.0 | A pause is not failure: save progress, make the task smaller, delegate, or request human contact by consent |
| 5 | Immediate Post-Death Guidance and Funeral-Period Coordination | 1.0 | Intentional transition to "recent loss"; acknowledge loss before tasks; one immediate focus |
| 6 | First-Year Bereavement, Estate, Benefits, and Life-Administration Navigation | 1.0 | Paced first-year view; prepare questions for professionals rather than answer them; remembrance and difficult dates |

### 5.1 Shared principles across the specs

- **Private by default.** Tasks, contacts, notes, documents, and support drafts start visible only to their creator or the Coordinator.
- **Partners see nothing by default.** A sponsoring hospice, insurer, or employer receives only a family-initiated request with exact fields previewed and consented (request ID, category, coordinator name, preferred contact method/time, optional message). It returns only limited status: received, acknowledged, contact attempted, connected, referred, closed.
- **Organizational role ≠ legal authority.** The Coordinator role never implies next-of-kin, executor, or decision-making status.
- **No inference-driven outreach.** Pauses, incomplete tasks, emotional check-ins, or financial stress never automatically notify family, partners, or emergency services.
- **No paid influence.** Recommendations are need-led, capped at four cards absent a request, and never ranked by placement, referral fees, sponsorship, or lead sales.
- **Pacing.** Every non-urgent step offers *Not now / Skip / Save*; no streaks, completion scores, or "overdue" badges.
- **Clear emergency boundary.** "Zeporah cannot monitor emergencies or provide emergency response," with routing to emergency services or the hospice's 24-hour line.

### 5.2 Role and permission model (Workflow 1)

| Role | Default access |
|---|---|
| Family Coordinator | Full administration; controls invitations, access, external consent |
| Co-Coordinator | Configurable elevated access; cannot remove original Coordinator by default |
| Family Helper | Assigned or explicitly shared items only |
| Family Viewer | Selected updates only, no editing |
| Partner Support Team | No workspace access; approved requests only |
| Zeporah Support | Limited, policy-governed operational access |

### 5.3 Journey stages and handoffs

Serious illness → Palliative care → Hospice care → Recent loss → Life after loss (plus "I am not sure"). Stage changes are always user-initiated, never time-triggered, and notify no one. Each handoff carries forward only what the next workflow needs and shows the family what will carry over; historic consent is never reused for a new external disclosure.

## 6. Cross-workflow audit — the "Trust Layer"

The audit concludes the six workflows form a credible end-to-end system but require eight foundational capabilities before launch:

1. Universal emergency, crisis, and safety layer (persistent "Get urgent help," quick exit)
2. Consent, permissions, and audit ledger (Privacy Center; "My Access" view)
3. State, county, and situation localization with editorial review and "last reviewed" dates
4. Accessibility, language, and low-tech access standard (16px minimum text, professional translation of safety and consent content)
5. Resource quality and provider-neutrality governance ("Zeporah financial relationship: None")
6. Financial-hardship and practical-support pathway, with no lending or upsell
7. Notification governance (generic previews, quiet hours, no "overdue" language)
8. Support triage and escalation model for the Zeporah team

It closes with a launch-readiness checklist spanning safety, content/localization, operations/data, and family experience.

## 7. Business strategy (KinPath-era documents)

*These documents predate the Zeporah name. The Revised Year-One Strategy, Immediate Next Steps Plan, and Five-Year Plan match the confirmed institution-first model most closely; the Brief's and Year 1 Execution Plan's family-direct sequencing is superseded.*

### 7.1 Market and competitive thesis

- ~3.07M U.S. deaths in 2024, projected to ~3.91M by 2045; death-care industry ~$23–24B; ~75% of funeral homes independent.
- Consumer subscriptions have failed in this category (Lantern, Everplans); scaled players sell B2B2C (Empathy via insurers and employers).
- Identified white space: full death-to-one-year coordination with *disclosed* vendor economics, and the largely unclaimed hospice channel.

### 7.2 How the strategy evolved

The plans move from expansive to deliberately narrow:

| Document | Wedge | Year-one ambition |
|---|---|---|
| Funeral & Estate Coordination App plan | Families direct in Georgia → hospices → employers/insurers | One-time packages ($99–$299), disclosed vendor fees, B2B2C later |
| Year-One Plan & Market-Cornering Strategy | "Next-of-kin operating system"; hospice compliance wedge | 1,000+ families, 15–20 paying hospices, 20–30 employers, $400–700K ARR, Benefits Recovery Engine, doula network |
| Five-Year Business Development Plan | Atlanta hospices, B2B2C | Year 1: 3–5 partners, 300–700 families; Year 5: $12–22M revenue (illustrative) |
| Revised Year-One Strategy | Hospice bereavement operations + family handoff | 3–5 paying hospices; responsive web only; staged capital ($5–25K → $50–150K → $450–650K) |
| Immediate Next Steps Plan | Same hospice thesis, operationalized | Day 90: 25–30 interviews, 5 workflow maps, 3 design partners, clickable prototype |
| Year 1 Execution Plan | Georgia families + local referrers, then hospice | 100–300 cases, 20–60 paid packages, $175–225K budget; interview scripts for six stakeholder groups |

The most recent and most conservative position (Revised Strategy and Next Steps, September 2026) reframes the initial product as **a bereavement-operations and family-guidance platform for hospices**, defers native apps, marketplaces, benefits filing, employer and hospital products, and renames the "Benefits Recovery Engine" to *Benefits Discovery and Claims Readiness*, with only verified payments counted as "recovered."

### 7.3 Recurring compliance guardrails

FTC Funeral Rule posture (refer and coordinate; do not sell or arrange); unauthorized-practice-of-law boundary (information, not advice; Texas §81.101(c)-style disclaimer adopted nationally); referral-fee bans in Washington and New Jersey; HIPAA status dependent on actual data flows rather than assumed; GLBA Safeguards and FTC Health Breach Notification Rule exposure; SOC 2 controls built early, audited when buyers require it.

## 8. Inconsistencies to resolve

These are the points where documents in the folder disagree. Each should be settled before the materials go to partners or developers.

1. **Name — resolved: Zeporah.** Remaining task: re-brand the six KinPath strategy documents or mark them as superseded.
2. **First customer — resolved: families direct at rollout.** This aligns with the Brief, deck, and Year 1 Execution Plan. Two follow-ons remain:
   - All six workflow specs list their *Primary release context* as "Hospice and palliative-care pilot." Workflow 1 already supports a family-created workspace with no partner connected, so the core flows work for direct families, but the release-context line, entry paths, and partner-queue sections should be re-framed so a family-direct launch is primary and the partner path is an extension.
   - The Revised Strategy and Next Steps plans contain a ready-made hospice discovery program (interview targets, scripts, a 40-prospect list, design-partner ask, pilot scorecard). Since hospice interest is real but unvalidated, that program can run in parallel with the family launch without changing the rollout.
3. **Product scope.** The Brief and deck describe "what comes after a death"; the brand foundation and Workflows 1–4 extend to serious illness and hospice care before death.
4. **Workflow taxonomy.** Three different structures appear: the deck's five MVP workflows, the six specification workflows, and the audit's six journey names (Care & Preparation, When Death Occurs, Honor & Remember, Estate & Life Administration, Family Coordination, Continuing Care). A mapping table, or a single naming scheme, would prevent confusion in build planning.
5. **Vendor economics.** Earlier plans and the Brief allow disclosed paid listings and fees (one plan proposes 5–7.5% featured placement); the workflow specs and audit prohibit paid placement, referral fees, and commissions entirely. These are materially different business models.
6. **Roles.** The Brief and deck use function-based helper roles (Financial, Logistics, Documents, Family Liaison, View Only); the specs use access-level roles (Co-Coordinator, Helper, Viewer). The Revised Strategy uses "workspace administrator"; the audit uses "Workspace Organizer."
7. **Hospice bereavement period.** The first business plan cites "up to 13 months"; later documents cite "up to one year" or "12 months." Worth confirming against 42 CFR §418.64 before it appears in partner materials.
8. **Spec versions.** Workflow 1 is v1.3 and Workflow 2 is v1.2; Workflows 3–6 are v1.0. The audit's required additions do not yet appear to be folded into the individual specs.
9. **Duplicates.** Each workflow exists in a plain and an illustrated edition with ~92–95% identical text, and the two brand PDFs are near-duplicates (full vs. condensed). Designating one canonical edition of each would reduce drift.
